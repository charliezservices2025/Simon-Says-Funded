Healthcare careers in Sacramento

The Sacramento healthcare workforce

Sacramento, in Sacramento County, sits within easy reach of our Orangevale campus, about 25 to 30 minutes southwest of our Orangevale campus by way of US-50 or Interstate 80. Its hospitals, clinics, skilled nursing facilities, and senior living communities depend on trained caregivers who hold current certifications, and demand keeps growing as the population ages. That is true for the Certified Nursing Assistants who provide hands-on patient care and for the clinical teams who are required to keep an American Heart Association BLS or ACLS card current.

The area around Sacramento is home to a deep healthcare community. Major hospitals in and around Sacramento include UC Davis Medical Center, Sutter Medical Center Sacramento, Mercy General Hospital, and the Kaiser Permanente Sacramento and South Sacramento medical centers, with Mercy San Juan Medical Center in nearby Carmichael. The area also supports skilled nursing and post-acute centers such as McKinley Park Care Center, Sacramento Post-Acute, and Saylor Lane Healthcare Center, along with senior living communities such as Carlton Senior Living. Large employers in the local healthcare landscape include UC Davis Health, Sutter Health, Dignity Health, and Kaiser Permanente, plus senior-care providers such as Eskaton and home health and hospice through Sutter Care at Home. You will find these teams working from Old Sacramento and the State Capitol to the Midtown medical district.

Pay and demand

Healthcare pay and job outlook near Sacramento

Healthcare support work is among the fastest-growing parts of the economy. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics projects healthcare support occupations, which include nursing assistants, to grow about 15 percent from 2023 to 2033, faster than any other occupational group. That demand shows up directly in the state capital region, where hospitals and care communities hire and retrain caregivers year round.

Pay reflects that demand. Nationally, the median wage for nursing assistants was about $39,530 a year, or roughly $19.84 an hour, as of May 2024, and California is among the highest-paying states, with a mean wage of around $44,700 a year, close to $21 an hour. A current CPR, BLS, or ACLS card is a common hiring requirement across these roles, which is one reason local teams keep their certifications up to date.

Wage and outlook figures are from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (Occupational Outlook Handbook, Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, and Employment Projections, 2023 to 2033). Wages vary by employer, shift, and experience and can change over time; confirm current figures before making decisions.
