## CNA wages and outlook (BLS)
- National CNA (Nursing Assistants, SOC 31-1131) MEDIAN annual wage: $39,530 (BLS OEWS/OOH, May 2024).
- National CNA MEAN hourly wage: $19.84 (BLS, May 2024).
- Nursing assistants employ ~1.4 million nationally; third-largest healthcare occupation (BLS, May 2024).
- California CNA mean wage: approx $44,700/year (about $21 per hour), among the highest in the nation
  (BLS OEWS May 2024, via aggregated OEWS state data). Phrase as "among the highest-paying states"
  and cite BLS OEWS; keep the figure approximate.
- Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom MSA all-occupation mean hourly: $36.66 (BLS, May 2024) [context only].
- Outlook: healthcare SUPPORT occupations (which INCLUDE nursing assistants) projected to grow
  15.2% from 2023 to 2033, the fastest-growing occupational group (BLS Employment Projections 2023-33).
  DO NOT attribute 15.2% to CNAs specifically; attribute to the group. CNAs grow steadily, driven by
  an aging population.
- Sources:

  - https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oes311131.htm
  - https://www.bls.gov/regions/west/news-release/occupationalemploymentandwages_sacramento.htm

## California CNA certification (CDPH) - established/verified in prior research
- Certifying agency: California Department of Public Health (CDPH), Aide and Technician Certification Section.
- Minimum state-approved training: at least 160 hours (minimum 60 hours classroom theory + 100 hours
  supervised clinical). Source: CDPH CNA program pages.
- State competency exam (written + skills) required after approved training; Live Scan fingerprint
  background check required.
- Renewal: every 2 years; 48 hours of continuing education per renewal period (in-service hours),
  with paid work as a nurse assistant required during the period.
- Source: https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CHCQ/LCP/Pages/CNA.aspx

## AHA BLS / ACLS
- AHA BLS and ACLS provider cards are valid for 2 years (renew before expiry).
- BLS: foundational CPR/AED for anyone; ACLS: advanced cardiac care for licensed clinicians (needs BLS first).
- Source: https://cpr.heart.org/
